# Cloxa: Belgian working-time registration, legal notes for product design

Last verified: 2026-09-27 (web research; sources listed at the end)

> **Disclaimer.** This is not legal advice. It is a product-research summary built from
> public sources (FOD WASO, RSZ, the DPA, CJEU, and publications by social secretariats
> and law firms). The rules are still moving: the general 2027 obligation is only a
> **draft**. Before relying on anything here for customer-facing claims, check it with a
> Belgian employment lawyer or social secretariat. "Unconfirmed" means we could not
> verify the point in an official or primary source.

---

## TL;DR

- **The "2027" claim is directionally correct but not yet law.** The federal budget
  agreement of Nov 2025 says "de registratie van arbeidstijd te verplichten vanaf 1
  januari 2027". A draft bill (voorontwerp) went to the National Labour Council
  (NAR/CNT) on 26 Mar 2026. As of Sep 2026 it had not been voted or published. Reported
  target: in force 1 Jan 2027, with a grace period until **1 Apr 2027** for employers
  that have no suitable system yet.
- **Registration is already mandatory today** for several groups:
  - sliding/flexible schedules ("glijdende uurroosters")
  - part-time workers who work outside their published schedule, or who have variable
    schedules
  - flexi-jobs
  - horeca attendance (GKS/ASA)
  - construction and cleaning site presence (Checkin@Work, and CIAO from 1 Apr 2027)
  - student hours (via Dimona and Student@work)
- **Recurring requirements:** the system must be "objective, reliable and accessible"
  (CJEU C-55/18). Records are kept **5 years**. Both the worker and the social
  inspection must be able to consult them.

## 1. Current legal obligations (in force today)

### 1.1 Sliding / flexible schedules ("glijdende uurroosters", art. 20ter Arbeidswet 16 Mar 1971)

- **Who:** employers where the worker chooses their own start and end times within a
  framework set in a CAO or in the work regulations (arbeidsreglement). The framework
  covers core hours, a 3-month reference period (up to 1 year), and a carry-over of 12
  hours.
- **What to record:** a time-registration system per worker with:
  - worker identity
  - daily duration of work
  - for part-timers on a fixed-schedule regime: start and end of the working day and of
    breaks

  The worker must be able to see their plus/minus balance against the average weekly
  duration over the reference period.

- **Access:** each worker concerned and the social inspection must be able to consult
  the system.
- **Retention:** 5 years after the day the data relates to.
- **Work regulations:** must describe the registration, retention and access
  arrangements.
- **Sanctions:** the Law of 15 May 2024 (Sociaal Strafwetboek, in force 1 Jul 2024)
  makes a missing or non-compliant system a **level 2** offence:
  - criminal fine EUR 400-4,000, or administrative fine EUR 200-2,000
  - multiplied by the number of workers affected (max 100)

  Missing work-regulation content is a **level 1** offence (admin EUR 80-800).

- Sources: Claeys & Engels, Securex (Jul 2024).

### 1.2 Part-time workers (Programmawet 22 Dec 1989 art. 157-169; KB 8 Mar 1990)

- **Variable schedules:**
  - The schedule must be communicated to each worker individually, in writing and dated,
    at least **7 working days** in advance (a sector CAO can lower this to a minimum of
    3).
  - It must go through a "reliable, suitable and accessible" means.
  - The notice stays available at the workplace (paper or electronic) while it is in
    effect, and is kept 1 year after that (FOD WASO).
- **Deviations from the schedule** must be recorded in one of two ways:
  - **Time-tracking system ("tijdsopvolgingssysteem"):**
    - Records per worker: identity, the period covered, and per day the start and end of
      work and of rest breaks.
    - Entries are recorded when the work starts or ends.
    - The workers concerned and the inspection must be able to consult it.
    - It does not have to be electronic; a mechanical clock is acceptable.
  - **Deviation document ("afwijkingsdocument"):**
    - Follows the KB 8 Mar 1990 model: name, date, actual start and end, breaks.
    - Filled in at the moment the work starts or ends.
- **Retention:** 5 years (paper or electronic).
- **Sanction:** a rebuttable presumption that the worker was employed **full-time**.
- Source: FOD WASO pages on part-time work.

### 1.3 "Nieuwe arbeidsregelingen" (Law of 17 Mar 1987 / CAO 42)

- These are procedural rules for introducing new work regimes: a CAO or procedure,
  schedules in the work regulations, and a register for worker remarks.
- **We found no separate time-registration obligation** specific to this regime (FOD
  WASO page).
- Related change: from **1 Jun 2026** (laws of 18 May 2026, Staatsblad 1 Jun 2026), work
  regulations may define a **framework of regular working hours** instead of listing
  every full-time or fixed part-time schedule.

### 1.4 Voluntary overtime (Law 18 May 2026, in force retroactively from 1 Apr 2026)

- Allowance: 360 hours per year (450 in horeca), of which 240 (360 in horeca) carry no
  overtime premium.
- Requires a written worker agreement that is valid 1 year and renews tacitly.
- Part-timers are only eligible under restrictive conditions.
- **We found no specific registration format** for these hours (FOD WASO update of 5 May
  2026). However, you cannot track the annual ceilings without time data.

### 1.5 Horeca: GKS and attendance registration

- **Who:** employers with a registered cash register system (GKS; mandatory above about
  EUR 25k per year in on-site meals).
- **What:** the daily start and end of attendance of staff in horeca establishments,
  recorded via the GKS or via the RSZ alternative system (ASA / "Personeelsbestand",
  linked to Dimona). The registration supplements Dimona; it does not replace it. Casual
  (occasional) workers and flexi-workers are excluded from this particular registration.
- **Status:** this registration was a condition for the horeca "vaste werknemers"
  target-group reduction. The Programmawet of **30 May 2026** (art. 120) abolished that
  reduction from 1 Jul 2026.
- **Unconfirmed:** whether the attendance registration remains a standalone obligation
  after 1 Jul 2026. Sources conflict and the social-secretariat articles we read are
  silent.

### 1.6 Flexi-jobs (Law 16 Nov 2015)

- **Dimona:**
  - Oral contract: a Dimona **per day**, including the start and end hour.
  - Written framework contract: a Dimona per quarter.
  - Start/end corrections have strict deadlines (e.g. a later end within 8 hours).
- **Time registration:** the employer must record and keep the start and end hour of
  each worker's work. Accepted means: day-Dimona, GKS, ASA, an electronic time-tracking
  system, or the digital tachograph (reported as now allowed in all sectors: Acerta via
  Legal News, 10 Sep 2026).
- **Sanctions:** reported as admin EUR 1,000-10,000 or criminal EUR 2,000-20,000 per
  worker (Acerta). **Unconfirmed** in a primary source.

### 1.7 Construction and similar: Checkin@Work, then "Check In and Out at Work" (CIAO)

- **Today:**
  - Checkin@Work: presence registration on construction sites of at least **EUR 500,000
    excl. VAT** (works in immovable property, ready-mix concrete delivery). It also
    covers the meat sector.
  - Cleaning of immovable property has used CIAO since **1 Sep 2024** (Programmawet 26
    Dec 2022).
- **From 1 Apr 2027** (Programmawet 30 May 2026, Title 6, Ch. 1, art. 124-131):
  - Construction (≥ EUR 500k) moves to CIAO with a mandatory **check-in and check-out**.
  - Each person present on site registers themselves in real time. Pre-registration is
    forbidden.
  - Checkin@Work will no longer accept construction registrations.
  - Breaks: Securex (Mar 2026) says they need not be registered "for now". The RSZ CIAO
    page mentions breaks. Treat as **unconfirmed / check the RSZ docs**.
- **Channels:** an RSZ online service (checkinout-management.socialsecurity.be).
  - **Unconfirmed:** whether a public API for vendors exists. Suivo (Jul 2026) says
    technical channels are "still being published".

### 1.8 Students (student@work)

- Contingent: **650 hours per calendar year** at reduced solidarity contributions
  (permanent since 2025). Hours above that are subject to normal contributions.
- Dimona "STU": for each quarter, the employer declares the **planned hours**. Students
  and employers check the balance via Student@work.
- There is **no separate daily time-registration obligation** specific to students
  beyond the general rules. However, real-vs-planned hours matter for Dimona
  corrections.

### 1.9 Temporary agency workers (Law 24 Jul 1987, art. 19)

- The **user company** is responsible for applying the working-time and
  labour-protection rules at the workplace. For working-time offences it is treated as
  the employer under the Sociaal Strafwetboek.
- Implication: interim workers must be registrable in the user's system. The agency
  usually needs the hours for payroll.

### 1.10 Telework

- **We found no specific working-time registration obligation** for telework today.
- The 2027 draft reportedly **excludes** home workers and teleworkers (Acerta, SD Worx,
  Partena FAQ). Unions (FGTB/ABVV) criticise this in light of CJEU Loredas.

### 1.11 Sector registrations not researched in depth

- Transport: tachograph and road-transport working-time rules. Out of scope; mentioned
  by Acerta as an existing obligation.

## 3. Data protection (GDPR / Belgian DPA, GBA/APD)

### Legal basis

- The legal obligation (art. 6(1)(c) GDPR) covers existing and upcoming registration
  duties.
- Otherwise the basis is contract necessity or legitimate interest.
- Consent in an employment relationship is generally **not "free"**, per the DPA.

### Biometrics (fingerprint or face clocks)

- Biometric data is a special category (art. 9 GDPR).
- The Belgian DPA says consent from employees is normally not valid. A legal basis must
  be grounded in national law and be proportionate.
- Central storage of templates is only justified "in exceptional cases". The template
  should stay with the worker, for example on a badge. See also DPA Recommendation
  01/2021.
- **Enforcement example:** Disputes Chamber decision **114/2024 (6 Sep 2024)**, a **EUR
  45,000 fine** for fingerprint time registration. There was no free consent, and no
  alternative was offered.
- **Product stance: do not offer biometric clock-in** in Belgium, or at minimum not as a
  default.

### Geolocation

- There is no specific Belgian law on geolocating workers; GDPR, art. 8 ECHR and art. 22
  of the Constitution apply.
- DPA principles:
  - **Legality:** set out in the work regulations after worker consultation.
  - **Legitimate purpose:** safety, vehicle protection, trip optimisation, checking
    performance.
  - **Proportionality:** no permanent tracking, no speed control, no tracking outside
    working hours (including commutes and breaks), and no tracking of workers who
    organise their own movements. It must be possible to switch it off.
  - **Transparency:** tell workers beforehand.
- **Product stance:** if location is ever used, capture it only as a point-in-time value
  at clock-in or clock-out. Make it opt-in per tenant, documented, and never continuous.

### Retention and minimisation

- Keep what the law requires (5 years under 1.1 and 1.2, likely under the 2027 law),
  then delete or anonymise.
- Do not keep extra telemetry (IP, device, GPS) longer than needed. The worker has
  access rights (art. 15 GDPR).

### Other

- The product needs a controller/processor agreement (DPA art. 28): Cloxa is the
  processor and the employer is the controller.
- Likely needs a DPIA for systematic monitoring of employees. This is our inference; the
  DPA page did not state it explicitly.

## 4. Social secretariat integration

- **No public, common Belgian standard format** for time or "prestaties" import was
  found. Each secretariat uses its own wage codes ("looncodes") and its own channels.
- Integrations are typically:
  - partner APIs (under agreement)
  - file uploads (CSV/Excel) via the client portal
  - bespoke connectors built by vendors (e.g. Strobbo, Officient, GPS Emprova, AFAS)

| Secretariat              | What is public                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **SD Worx**              | International "SD Worx Connect" APIs and standard file formats. The Belgian **HR Selfservice webservice** is read-only for **absences**, uses OAuth, and is activated via support@sdworx.com. A public prestaties-import spec was **not found**. |
| **Acerta**               | "REST APIs or file exchange". Axway-based integration/developer portal launched in 2025. A partner programme exists (>100 partners). Access is by contact.                                                                                       |
| **Securex**              | Partner marketplace (hrpartners.securex.be) with a "technical integration" category and a time-registration category. No public spec; access by becoming a partner.                                                                              |
| **Partena Professional** | Integration tool announced for cloud HR/payroll. Several time-tracking partners send prestaties "in a few clicks". A public spec was **not found**.                                                                                              |
| **Liantis**              | Monthly prestaties are sent via My Liantis ("Prestaties doorsturen" tile). Vendors (Strobbo, GPS) integrate. A public spec was **not found**.                                                                                                    |
| **Group S**              | Vendors list it as a supported target (AFAS, Strobbo). No public spec found.                                                                                                                                                                     |

- **Design implication:** build a **pluggable export layer**. Map internal time
  categories to per-tenant wage codes. Start with a configurable CSV export and add
  secretariat-specific connectors later, via their partner programmes.

## 5. Product implications

### Must-have

1. **Per-worker registration of actual working time:** date, start, end, and breaks
   (start/end or duration), timestamped when the event happens. This meets the strictest
   existing regimes (part-time deviations, flexi, sliding schedules). It is a superset
   of the likely "deviations only" 2027 option.
2. **Schedule baseline and deviation mode:** store the published or planned schedule.
   Support recording only deviations (the likely 2027 option), with an actual-hours mode
   as default or recommended.
3. **Immutable audit trail:** the original entry is never overwritten. Corrections are
   separate records with who, when, why and approval. The system must be "objective and
   reliable" and must not be silently editable by the employer.
4. **Worker access:** a worker can view their own records and balances, including the
   plus/minus against the average for sliding schedules, and can export them.
5. **Inspection access:** a fast export per worker and period (PDF plus CSV), with the
   full correction history.
6. **Retention:** keep at least **5 years** after the date the data relates to.
   Configurable purge after that. Archived data stays exportable after an employee
   leaves.
7. **Part-time schedule notices:** individual, dated schedule communication at least 7
   working days ahead (configurable minimum of 3). Keep them 1 year after they stop
   being in effect.
8. **Balance and limit counters:**
   - sliding-schedule reference period and 12-hour carry-over
   - voluntary overtime 360/240 (horeca 450/360) per calendar year
   - student planned hours per quarter vs. actual, with the 650-hour context
9. **Worker types:** employee, part-time (fixed or variable), flexi (per-day start/end
   must match Dimona), student, interim (at the user company), and excluded categories
   (flag, not delete).
10. **Tenant settings for the work-regulations text:** describe the system, retention
    and access. Offer a template, not legal advice.
11. **Privacy by default:** no biometrics, no continuous GPS, an optional point-in-time
    location per tenant, minimal device data. DPA/processor agreement, a sub-processor
    list, and EU hosting.
12. **Payroll export:** per-tenant wage-code mapping and a monthly prestaties export
    (CSV first).
13. **Clock correctness:** server-side timestamps, Europe/Brussels time zone, DST
    handling, and overnight shifts that cross midnight (matters for flexi Dimona).

### Must NOT claim

- That time registration **"is mandatory for all employers from 1 Jan 2027"** as settled
  law. It is a **draft**. Say "planned from 1 Jan 2027 (draft law, not yet adopted)".
- "Fully compliant with the 2027 law" or "inspection-proof": the final text, data fields
  and sanctions are unknown.
- That Cloxa replaces **Dimona**, **Checkin@Work/CIAO**, the **GKS**, or tachographs.
  These are separate government or sector channels, unless an integration is actually
  built and certified.
- That paper or Excel is "illegal" (unconfirmed), or that a time clock is required (it
  is explicitly not).
- That biometric clock-in is GDPR-compliant in Belgium.
- Legal advice, or that we "handle" the employer's legal obligations. The employer
  remains the controller and remains responsible.
- Official partnership or certified integration with SD Worx, Securex, Partena, Acerta,
  Liantis or Group S, unless one exists.
